More and more hospitals and clinics are using AI to write first drafts of replies to patient messages, like the ones sent through MyChart. A doctor or nurse reviews the draft and sends it under their own name, but because they’re busy, they often approve it quickly, and patients usually aren’t told AI was involved. 

We tend to think a message is shaped by two people: the one who writes it and the one who reads it. With AI, there’s a third influence: the software itself. AI has its own habits that affect a message’s wording, tone, length, and what gets left out. A hospital may only want to save time, but the AI can create effects nobody intended, like sounding overly confident or skipping a patient’s follow-up question. When drafts are approved quickly, those choices reach patients mostly unchanged. 

That’s why AI should be seen as an active part of the conversation, not an invisible tool.
