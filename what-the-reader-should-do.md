As a patient, you can protect yourself by checking important details, like medicines, doses, and dates, against your own records, and by focusing on what a message actually says rather than how friendly it sounds. If a reply feels generic, ask follow-up questions, ask whether AI helps write the messages, and request a phone call or visit if the news is serious. 

Doctors and nurses should check AI drafts for tone and unanswered questions, not just facts, and should support telling patients when AI is used. AI can be a helpful way to save time, but it should never replace a doctor’s judgment, compassion, and attention to each patient’s situation. Used carefully, it can lighten doctors’ workloads without costing the trust good patient care depends on.
